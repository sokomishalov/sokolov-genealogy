RUDNERWEIDE MOLOTSCHNA

as translated by Eric Francis Garbin March 1998
Coordinated with GRHS Village Research Clearing House
Coordinated with GRHS Translation Committee Chairman

Rudnerweide

Whenever one climbs the gently rising hill which is separated from the northeast
side of the colony by a small valley, one can easily survey  the two rows of
houses of Rudnerweide. The houses are regularly placed, suitably erected, and of
pleasing appearance.

Further enhancing these house exteriors are the regularly interspersed orchards,
which are bordered along the lane with wild pear-trees, between neighbor and
neighbor with a mulberry hedge and on the end to the rear with a hedge of wild
olive-trees. When one turns towards the northeastern end of the colony, one sees
a  two-story white stone building covered with white dutch rooftiles, in
addition to a respectably prospering newly-planted forest.

This building is the prayer house for the entire Rudnerweide congregation )2),
for whose construction Kaiser Alexander the First gave 10,000 Russian banknotes.
Somewhat further to the southeast are located the planted woods for the entire
community, one-third mulberry trees, which yield so many leaves that every year
each landlord can make 1 Pud of silk. The plantation comprises 16 1/2 dessiatine
of surface area. Of this, each landlord possesses a quarter of 1/2 dessiatine.
Each quarter is surrounded by mulberry hedges, the whole plantation with a wild
olive hedge perimeter. West of this plantation lies the new windscreen hedgerow,
which extends from one end to the other on the colony's south side, serving to
protect the orchards behind it from storms and blizzards.

At the southwest end of the colony, the renters live in likewise orderly
constructed, orchards ringed houses. Among the buildings there are a few
stand-outs: besides the prayerhouse, the massive two-story building of the
vinegar-brewery, nearest to a one-story residence of white stone; a massive
one-story beer brewery on the right side of the valley, which takes a southerly
direction here. All of these buildings are covered with clutch roof tiles.

Rudnerweide was founded in 1820. The first dwellings were erected in a section
of newly-built livestock stalls, while at the same time, the building of houses
was readied. The colony is not far from the steppe river Juchanlee along the
length of the valley Sassikulak. In the south and the southwest, the colony is
bordered by the Juchanlee,  to the north by the Tschumaken road, to the east by
the Grossweide colony.

The prairie has no depression, and the water level is thirty to fifty feet
below. The fertile ground consists of humus. When left fallow, it becomes so
loose and fine, that the intense storms tear it away and create deep furrows.
Below ground one may extract good clay for bricks, and the common field stone in
Sassikulak valley is used for foundations.

Most settlers of this colony were drawn from the Rudnerweide village in Prussia.
The naming of that village had the following source: upon the height which
dominates the east side of the Vistula valley, lies the big village Ruden. The
Rudener possessed livestock meadows ("Viehweiden") below the Vistula years
before. When thereupon a village was laid out, it received the name
"Rudnerweide," which has been carried over to this colony as well.

Originally, twenty-four immigrating families from the Prussian official district
of Stuhm settled in this colony. In 1826, however, more families arrived from
the Marienburg and Marienwerder districts to join them. )3)

Most of the first-settlers belonged to the congregation of the churchelder,
Franz Goerz, which in 1835 was an already substantial group. [From this
congregation,] not only these, but [also] various neighboring colonies have been
populated, which can be regarded as daughter-colonies to Rudnerweide and
according to most sources, to the congregation of Goerz (and his successor,
Benjamin Ratzlaff) as well. )4)  The local steppe was of course used by the
Tartars for their own herds, and was leased by Johann Kornies.

As most of the settlers were poor, they got an advance loan from the crown of
12,524 Russian banknotes. A few of the original and subsequently settled
families had sufficient means amounting to about 30,000 Russian banknotes.

The further historical progress of this colony exhibits no features diverging
from those of other colonies.

Mayor:  Peter Kliewer.
Committee Members: Andreas Nachtigal, Gerhard Fast.
Schoolteacher: Jakob Brauel.

April 25, 1848.

F O O T N O T E S ;

)1) cf. Koeppen: Ueber einige Landesverhaeltnisse [Concerning certain land
relationships] page 61ff.

)2) The Rudnerweide Congregation belonged to the Friesian branch, cf. Friesen a.
a. O. page 706.

)3) 1855: 33 farmsteads, 67 renter families (in total 278 men, 256 women), cf.
Mennonitische Blaetter Vol. 4, 1857,page 31 (including those absent).  1857: 33
farmsteads, (190 men) on 2145 Desi. and 25 landless families (100 men), cf.
Klaus a. a. O. Beilage 2, page 37.

)4) Benjamin Ratzlaff, born 1791, carder to elderhood in 1837, was still serving
in 1856, cf. Mennonitische Blaetter Vol. 4, 1857, page 32.
