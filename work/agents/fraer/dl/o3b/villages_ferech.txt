January 19, 1929


Church Happenings since 1911

by Pastor A. Simsont
  (Translated from the German to English by Horst Gutsche - 1998)

  On January 14. 1929, it was 18 years since I came to Fere Champenoise(1) for
  the first time. The Honorable Evangelical Lutheran Consistory in St.
  Petersburg had designated me as vicar of Fere Champenoise parish, which was
  vacant at the time.  After I had preached in all the parish villages, I was
  elected unanimously on February 18, 1911 in a parish meeting led by Pastor
  Arthur Hanson of Arcis.

  It seems important for me to say something about myself. I was born on July 31,
  1863 in Oppekaln - Livonia and when baptized received the name Johann Karl
  Alexander. I received my first instruction at home, then in the elementary and
  then the regional school in Walk. I completed my gymnasium studies (high
  school). I took this exam in 1890 in St. Petersburg. I had my vicarage for the
  preaching ministry in the German colony Lessno - Karamysch (Grimm) municipality
  of Saratov. Then I was pastor for 10 years (1898 - 1908) in Platbust
  Municipality of Uta. Then pastor in Lasdohn, Livonia from where I moved in
  January 1911 to Fere Champenoise.

My call Document in Fere Champenoise included the following information:

  We the signers and representatives of the Evangelical Lutheran Parish of Fere
  Champenoise, province of Bessarabia, herewith call Pastor Alexander Simmont as
  our parish preacher and promise as soon as our election according to Article
  412 of the 1st part of the 11th volume of the Book of the Law of the Empire
  (1896 edition) has become judicially valid, to provide for Pastor A. Simsont,
  the following benefits income and provisions without reduction)

 1. We will pay for the support of our called and from a high authority confirmed
  pastor, the yearly sum of 1200 rubles (2600 Mark?) ($1508?)

 2. Our Pastor is to have the unencumbered use of 120 Desjatine (324 acres) of
  parish land and 5 1/2 acres of garden land. If the pastor relinquishes the
  administration of parish lands to a commission elected by the parish, then we
  commit ourselves having the pastor receipt all income, however if this income
  over a period of 5 years should produce less than 1500 rubles (3161 Reichmark)
  ($1,833) yearly, then we commit ourselves to augment it, that it will be at
  least 1500 rubles on a yearly basis.

 3. We commit ourselves to keep the manse at all times in a good state of repair
  as well as the out buildings and to carry out all repairs promptly at our cost.

 4. For the heating of the manse we will pay our pastor 160 rubles yearly ($183).

 5. In terms of ministerial fees, the pastor of the parish has the right to ask
  for the following: For every wedding which takes place in the church 1 ruble.
  For every church certificate 60 Kopecks.

 6. For all ministerial acts outside of the parish village as well as for al1
  church festivals and conferences within Bessarabia the pastor receives free
  transportation, (horse and buggy).

 7. For the trip to superintending synodical meetings the pastors transportation
  costs will be paid.

Fere Champenoise the 18th of February 1911.

 The signatures of the representatives of the six congregations are listed:
    Fere Champenoise I and II, Paris, Katzbach, Dennewitz, and Plotzk.

  The confirmation followed in June of the same year, so that I could move into
  the manse together with my family on June 29, 1911. In September 1911 the
  honorable General Superintendent Guide Pingood of St. Petersburg together with
  the honorable Superintendent Johannes Alber from Grossliebental undertook a
  visitation trip through the parishes of South Russia. On September 24th the
  General Superintendent arrived in Fere Champenoise I and that afternoon held a
  sermon on II Corinthians 7:6. On the same day The General Superintendent drove
  to Tarutino, while Superintendent Alber remained behind in order to introduce
  (install) me on the next day. The installation took place on September 25th
  1911 in the presence of Pastor Alphons Meyer of Sarata and Arthur Hanson from
  Arcis. I as the one who was installed preached from Romans 15 29-33.

  Since I had often met and gotten to know German colonists from Bessarabia on my
  pastoral visits in the province of Ufa and Orenburg and in the Turgai region, it
  was not difficult for me to feel comfortable in my new situation, After a time I
  got to know the people here and respected and treasured some of them. Several,
  church council members belonged to this group during my time as pastor here. I
  would like to name them here. Johannes Klotzbucber in Fere Champenoise I, who
  died on June 29, 1924. Johann Quast also in Fere Champenoise I, who died on
  January 8, 1926. Michael Knopp in Katzbach. Immanuel Ruge and Daniel Radke in
  Fere Champenoise II. Andreas Blum and Michael Frank in Plotzk - lay
  preacher/teachers were in the last years in Fere Champenoise I. Johann Witt
  until 1916, K. Arlt 1918 - 20, Emil Witt 1920-24, Christian Fruck since 1925. In
  Paris: Andreas Kehrer 1902-12 (died), Martin Weiss 19l2-1920, Reinhold Wagner
  1920-21, Johann Eichelberg since 1921. In Katzbach: Gustav Witt until 1911,
  Jakob Hehr 1911-12, Eduard Thilemann 1912-15, Nathaniel Thilemann 1915-16,
  Eduard Thilemann 1917, Rudolph Tschritter 1918-21, Rudolph Knauer 1921-27,
  Arnold Winger 1922-26,Rudolph Tschritter since 1926. In Fere Champenoise II:
  Johann Keck 1896-1911, Alexander Renz 1911-15, a temporary replacement 1915-17,
  Rudolph Tobler 1917-18, Jakob Hehr 1919-22, Daniel Radke 1918-19, 1922-24, Jakob
  Semmler 1924-25, Jakob Schaal 1925-27, Rudolph Oswald since 1927. in Dennewitz:
  Daniel Broneske 1908-18 Gottlieb Hahn 1918-21, David Weisspfennig 1921-22,
  Immanuel Radke 1922-24. Gotthold Hohloch since 1924. In Plotzk: Johannes Blum
  1907-22, temporary replacement Samuel Schwandt, A Keller, Gustav Lang, Immanuel
  Flaig, Herbert Mauch 1923-27 Johann Wacker since 1927. In Neu Paris: temporary
  replacement Karl Beierle, Christian Kruger, Immanuel Moritz, Paul Traichel,
  Heinrich Frieske 1921-25 D. Timm 1925-26, Theodore Schaible 1927-28.

  Of the named lay-preacher teachers the following can be especially mentioned
  as diligent co-workers of the pastor. Johann Wits, Mart Weiss, Alex Renz, Dan
  Broneske, and Johann Blum.

  After having named certain persons with whom I came into contact with. I would
  like to continue by giving a short description of the life in the congregation
  of the parish.

  No one lives unto himself, Romans 14:7. This word of the Apostle Paul proves
  itself in the life of individual parishes and congregations. They not only walk
  together in customs, traditions and viewpoints but also are dependent upon each
  other. In church life the active interest for the religion of the forefathers
  must be emphasized.

  The worship services are for the most part well attended, even if there are
  individual persons who seldom or never attend worship. All in all, church
  interest is quite general in the German villages of Bessarabia.

  Common goals are also showing force in the outward life of the parish. Before
  the war 1914-18, it was noted that there was a general desire for much land in
  the villages. Land was purchased wherever one could possibly purchase it. Since
  there was not enough money for this, banks were established. These banks were
  established from larger financial institutions so there could be enough money to
  borrow. Banks such as these were opened in Paris and Dennewitz.  With the
  outbreak of the war, this activity was reduced and within the second year of the
  war, they had to liquidate.  The debts which remained could be easily cancelled
  later on, when the value of money dropped, so that the purchase' price was low
  compared to the general prices. In this way, some attained land and wealth in a
  cheap and easy way.

  The war (1914-18) brought much unrest and bitterness in our congregations. All
  able bodied men were called into active service. The older men were enlisted for
  the digging of trenches. In the villages one seldom saw a young man. Only old
  and sick persons remained at home. During the muster and also during various
  quotas, there were often personal interests, which played an interest, whereby
  enmity and jealously developed among the village habitants.

  After the war, the Russian revolution also affected us; _ At least in the early
  beginnings of it, in the years 1917-18, we got .. a taste of it. It was a time
  of great uncertainty. This situation continued even when Rumania had already
  begun to take over individual sections of Bessarabia. March 17, 1918 was the day
  of union for Bessarabia with Rumania. As time went by, especially after the
  Liberal Party took over the government in Rumania, the revolutionary spirit was
  stopped, which had awakened after the war and showed itself in thieving and
  lawlessness.

  As in times of peace the desire for land was great. So, in the time that the war
  ended, this desire rose to a true state of miserliness, since the government had
  promised land to the soldiers when the war was over. The Rumanian government
  hurried in order to bring a certain lawful reason into the division of land. The
  House of Representatives passed a law and a special government bureau the "Casa
  Noastra" (our house) was founded which would undertake the expropriation.
  Whoever had more than 100 hectares (247 acres) was expropriated in return for a
  promised cash settlement. Which has not taken place yet. The land was given to
  landless families at the rate of 6 Hectares (15 acres approx.) per family.
  Schools were also to receive the same amount of land. Many well to do families
  in Fere Champenoise II, Dennewitz and Plotzk were affected by the law. The
  parish land also came in danger of being expropriated. Since according to the
  law, the pastor only received 15 acres, the same each for the deacon and the lay
  preacher/teacher, the parish only had 45 acres for use in the years 1920-22, The
  pastor received 30 acres and the lay preacher/teacher 15. The rest of the land
  was divided among the landless. In Fare Champenoise I this consisted of about 80
  families. Later it was effected that the lay preacher/teacher of the other
  villages also received 15 acres.

  Through the efforts of Friedrich Heer from Paris, acting as the representative
  of our parish, the Municipal Commission of the "Casa Noastra" in Cetetea Alba
  (Akkerman) decided on the 23rd of September 1922, that al 6 villages Of our
  parish receive 45 acres each. This was later confirmed by the Central Commission
  in Kischinew. Since 1922 the pariah owns 267 acres of Pastor's land, or more
  rightly stated church land. Of this land in 1922, each lay preacher/teacher
  received 15 acres, each church 15 acres, and the pastor of each congregation 15
  acres. Altogether 9O acres.

  The church and partially the lay preacher/teacher land had to be leased by the
  pastor, if he wanted to get something. The congregation paid no salaries
  anymore. It was a hard BDC - EWZ Filmstime. In the year 1924-26, I was forced to
  make special agreements with each congregation and to serve only for what could
  be earned from the lands. Since 1926 all the pastor's land which the Casa
  Noastra returned, except for 15 acres which belong to the lay-preacher in Paris
  has returned for the support of the pastor. As of January 1, 1926, the pastor
  receives a salary of 26,000 Lei.

  During the war and the revolution it was revealed how hard hearted the people
  could be ~ to their pastor. "Let him see to it how he gets through." At that
  time this was the common statement which was made. Burning material was also
  denied so .. that often his manse was unbearably cold. At the time when Russian
  soldiers were quartered, a captain moved into the manse, then it became warm
  again. Yes, hunger and cold have taught how little the people tend to keep their
  duties vis a vis their pastor which they had accepted and in written form had
  been signed (sealed). They dryly stated that their wills and letters of purchase
  were not accepted by the Casa Noastra' at the time of the expropriation either.
  So because of a forced government act, morality declines.

  I come back to the already mentioned word "No one lives unto himself." I must
  emphasize that the parish of Fere Champenoise always took part whenever the
  German community of Bessarabia had an event. Before the war there was a Brethren
  Conference in Kulm on May 20, 1912, a Social Service Account Festival in Fere
  Champenoise I on November 13 1912. The Superintendent Synod in Tarutino on
  September 8, 1913. These events were especially valuable in order to strengthen
  the collective consciousness. On the other hand, special state holidays were
  celebrated in order to emphasize the fact of national belonging. Such holidays
  were the Hundredth Celebration on May 16, 19l2, of the union of Bessarabia with
  Russia and on February 21, 19l3 the anniversary of the Russian royal family the
  Romanovs. The same took place when the Bessarabian governor Hilcher was in Fere
  Champenoise I on June 21, 1913 in order to increase patriotism. Before the war,
  church and patriotic feelings in the congregation were alive and therefore can
  be considered satisfactory.

  When the war began in August 1914, we didn't think that such destruction would
  take place, as was later evident. Even though the parish did not become an
  active place of combat, everything did begin to fall apart. Specifically, there
  was a moral fall. The terms of right and wrong were confused. Politicians also,
  who should constantly keep a clear view, were called to and fro by the feelings
  generated by the war. So in July 1916 the strange decree was given, that
  preaching in German was forbidden. As if such rules could help lead to victory!
  The Almighty God had to pull down the imperial throne of Russia so that this
  stupid law could be reversed. Emperor Nicholas II abdicated on March 2, 1917 and
  right away in March, the so called L'wow interim government allowed the use of
  the German language for preaching, (That is worship services) We believed that
  the time of freedom had arrived. In actuality though it was only the beginning
  of the revolution. The fights for freedom temporarily ignored the church. We
  tried, even though the war continued to anew somehow, especially since the new
  government had not established itself as yet, and therefore had ovated no
  barriers. Congresses were held or more correctly meetings. At first in Odessa,
  then also in Tarutino where negotiations were held regarding land units for
  church and school. The decisions of these congresses were for the most part
  stillborn. That is, they were never put into effect. During this time the
  Bessarabian Republic was separated from Russia, governed by a (-?-) in
  Kischinew. On March 27, 1918 the state assembly declared the union of Bessarabia
  with Rumania. Immediately thereafter the Rumanian occupation took place in our
  village. In regards to the Evangelical Lutheran Church in Bessarabia. The Church
  was cut off from the rest of the Evangelical Lutherans in Russia to which she
  had hitherto belonged. Church life continued in its usual form but the uncertain
  situation had its effect. In order to give the church a proper legal place, the
  pastors met in conference on January 20th 1919 in Tarutino. It was decided to
  ask the Rumanian government to confirm a newly formed Consistory led by Pastor
  Daniel Haase. Which was approved. The congregations were amazed at this
  independent "action" of the pastors. Only at the time of the first Bessarabian
  Synod meeting of November 28-30, 1920, was the Consistory somehow recognized,
  also by the congregations whose representatives elected members of the
  Consistory and set salaries.

  In the first years after the war, war memorials were built in many villages. The
  dedication of the memorials was a special celebration for the family members and
  village neighbors of the dead or missing. In Fere Champenoise II where the
  memorial cost 12,000 lei, was dedicated on October 22, 1922. In Fere Champenoise
  I a war memorial which cost 4,000 lei was dedicated on September 18, 1921. For
  both dedications, Pastors Daniel Haase and Rudolph Meyer from Arcis were present
  in Fere Champenoise II. Also, the father of the latter, pastor emeritus Alphons
  Meyer. The celebration was held in high spirits and worthy services. In this
  time the Social Security fund festival also took place on November 27, 1921.
  This festival was celebrated in Fere Champenoise I and attended by many pastors
  and church members.

  May I be able to write a bit more about a celebration? It is my 25th anniversary
  of ordination on April 29, 1923. We, that is my wife and I had decided to let
  the time of my 25th anniversary pass without notice. Because by this time I had
  only served 12 1/2 years in Fere Champenoise. Several congregational members
  however had been made aware of the situation by other pastors. They prepared
  without my knowledge, a celebration on a large scale. An anniversary committee
  had been elected which was to plan the celebration and take care of invitations.
  The day began with a selection played by the local orchestra under the direction
  of the brothers Gottfried and Immanuel Reinke. Twenty five tolls of the bell
  from the church tower announced to the congregation the meaning of the day. The
  worship attendance was high. Guests from other villages in the parish also came.
  I preached on II Corinthians 5:20. The service was followed by a banquet which
  was arranged by the parish. At this time Senior Pastor Daniel Haase presented a
  letter of congratulations from the Consistory, which read as follows. To the
  honorable Pastor Alexander Simsont, Fare Champenoise I. Through God's gracious
  leading it is your privilege most honored anniversary pastor to celebrate today
  the 15th anniversary of the work of your pastoral office. The Consistory found
  out with joy that this your personal celebration should not be restricted to a
  celebration in your own family circle. It should be celebrated as a community
  festival of all congregations in the parish and its pastors. This type of a
  celebration is not only a joyful sign of good will towards the pastor himself,
  but at the same time evidence of the way that the spiritual office is
  appreciated as well as the high benefits which this office administers. The
  Consistory adds its hearty wishes for success and blessings to those Of this
  congregation. May you be allowed to carry out your office for the salvation of
  the congregations for many years. May God carry this. April 29, 1923. President
  W. Mutschall, Vice President D. Haase, Secretary August Erdmann.

From the anniversary committee I received the following:

  Highly honored anniversary Pastor! The honored duty has been given to me to
  congratulate you in the name of your parish on the celebration of your 25th
  anniversary. May God grant, that you may remain a long time in our midst and
  grant you good health and freshness of spirit and body to carry out your holy
  office. This act which is meant to be a gift from your parish and which I ask
  that you accept as a sign of our friendship, to be a remembrance of this day for
  you. A remembrance that your parish takes the most lively interest in the
  happenings of your office and family life. God bless you! Signed by the
  secretary of the Anniversary Committee. The gift was a good sized lot in the
  church yard. At the banquet there were many uplifting speeches. The school
  children also sang a song which teacher C. Foler had written and F. Eichelberg
  had set to music,

The text was:

  Greeted, congratulated be our Jubilar, today, also by us, the happy group of
  children We wish luck on this beautiful day As well as word and song and
  announce it To the pastor, our senior jubilee person We wish this to you
  diligently from thy heart And want to prey Holy God to you For our dear pastor
  we all Never allow dear Lord, for him to suffer May he be seen in our midst for
  a long time Protect us from accident, illness need In your protection so as us
  him O God

  By means of a service in the afternoon at 5 o'clock in which Senior Pastor D.
  Haase-Tarutino and Pastor R. Meyer from Arcis preached, the celebration ended.

  Luther said: "Where schools are built, there a golden age has begun." A golden
  year also began in the village Fere Champenoise I. The old school building was
  demolished and a new one was built from the foundation up. The old school
  building was probably built in 1852, as Pastor Jordan reports in the chronicle.
  It had served as a prayer and school house. Since 1892 the Evangelical Lutheran
  Church school was situated in the building, with the Russian language being the
  medium of instruction. In the year 1917, the Russian soldiers were quartered in
  the building which led to a lot of destruction. For instance, the ceiling was
  torn out, the seats were dragged away, etc. After the occupation of Bessarabia
  by the Rumanians in 1918, the Rumanian government took over the salary of the
  teachers and declared the school in Fere Champenoise I as well as the other
  German schools in Bessarabia, to be state schools. They had belonged to the
  Semtswe schools during Russian times. At the same time, the Rumanian language
  was dictated as the medium of instruction. So the old school building had
  experienced varied times. In April 1924 it was completely torn down. On may 25th
  of the same year, the cornerstone for the building of a four classroom school
  was laid. This cornerstone is from the church side in the left corner of the
  front side of the schoolhouse. My devotion at this time was based on Psalm 127.
  The building of this schoolhouse was under the leadership of architect Chr.
  Jerke from Dennewitz. It was quickly accomplished. The congregation willingly
  and gladly paid the many dues exacted so that the building was completely
  finished by the beginning of October. On October 12th, the Joyful dedication
  took place.

  This day was also set as Harvest Thanksgiving. Pastor R. Meyer from Arcis came
  to the celebration. The congregation gathered for worship in the afternoon in
  the schoolyard before the locked door of the new building. First of all I
  preached as the pastor loci. (local preacher) I showed the parents, children,
  and teachers their duties and responsibilities in light of the new building.
  Thereupon Pastor R. Meyer held a speech in which he pointed out, that as
  everywhere, so also especially in school, everything depends on God's blessing.
  The chairman of the school committee Simon Reinke now opened the front door by
  accepting the keys from the architect. In one of the classrooms I proceeded with
  the dedication and spoke the prayer. After the dedication celebration there was
  a banquet in which the school inspector Teodorescu and the sub prefect from
  Arcis, Mr. Brussow partook. The school had cost 775,000 lei.

  In the same year-1924-there were two conferences in Tarutino. On June 1st, there
  was a meeting of the brethren and on September 16th a pastoral conference.

  The Bessarabian Synod was held in Tarutino on August 23, 1925 where the
  negotiations took place in regard to the union with the Evangelical church of
  Augsburg Confession (Lutheran Church) of Tranyslavania.

  The year 1925 brought us another celebration. It was the 100th anniversary of
  the founding of Fere Champenoise II. ~ In the year. 1825, 63 families that had
  originally settled in Fere Champenoise I, moved to the far end of the Fere
  Champenoise land and founded a new village there. In order to properly honor the
  event, the descendants of the 63 families set up a celebration of remembrance
  which took place on December 27, 1925. The celebration was begun as a church
  feast, with a worship service before noon, at which I gave a speech. The Senior
  Pastor D. Haase held the festival sermon on the basis of Deutoronomy 32: 1-10.
  That afternoon Pastor Winger from Sarata preached, as well as Pastor Meyer from
  Arcis. In the evening I spoke on the history of the village. This was followed
  by a community evening meal in which Immanuel Romppel lectured on the cultural
  development of the colony, Followed by Immanuel Broneske who lectured on the
  economic development of the colony Fere Champenoise II, A good time was had with
  lively songs and music. This lasted until late at night.

  On June 12, 1927 I dedicated the church tower in Paris and at the same time the
  war memorial. The former had cost 123,088 lei and the latter 19,940 lei. It was
  the work of the mayor of Paris, Friedrich Heer, that the clock was obtained and
  that the memorial was dedicated.

  In the summer of 1927 the church in Fere Champenoise I was renovated. This work
  was carried out in a very satisfactory manner through construction foreman
  Biriakow from Sarata. It cost 175,000 lei. On October 2nd we entered the newly
  renovated church. On the same day we celebrated Thanksgiving. On September
  23-26, 1927 the III Synod took place in Tarutino. The Synod was very stormy.
  Since many congregations were upset about the union of the until then (Lutheran)
  Regional Church of Bessarabia with the Evangelical Church of Augsburg Confession
  (Lutheran) in Tranyslvania, which had been effected by Senior Pastor D. Haase
  and Peter Wolleyt. Because of this union they refused to elect delegates for the
  synod according to the Tranyslvania church order. Only the church of Katzbach
  elected its delegates for the synod according to the new order. But, also, the
  lawfully elected representatives when they came together for the Synod were only
  willing to accept the Tranyslvania church order, "if", 11 points which they
  listed would be accepted. Through their disputes there were many undesirable
  animosities brought into church matters.  Because of this, many church pastors
  had to suffer. Many had been accused of dictatorial views. A special Synod held
  on April 18th, 19th, 1928 was not able to change the bad atmosphere.

  In many cases this and other disputes and the party spirit damaged church life
  in some villages. For instance in, Fere Champenoise II. There church council
  member Johann Irion was accused of having wanted to lead the wife of Adam
  Schulz, into an immoral act, during the war, when he was in military service and
  absent. Voices were raised for and against him. A complaint was sent to the
  Consistory which also took the matter in hand with youthful vigor. The accuser
  A. Schulz and his following were disappointed in the position of the Consistory
  and dropped their accusation.  Only when Johann Irion resigned from his position
  as church council member in February 1921.  Peace did return to the church, but
  in the brethren meetings the division because of the dispute continues.

  In Plotzk, the dispute which developed because of lay preacher/teacher Johannes
  Blum added much to the fact that church life began to suffer. A group had formed
  which would not tolerate him, because he stuck his nose into congregational
  affairs. However, since he had a large relationship in Plotzk he was powerfully
  supported by them. As long as he was lay preacher/teacher the followers of the
  opposition group only came to worship when the preacher held services.

  Of the substitute lay preacher/teacher, Samuel Schwandt came into disrepute. He
  was in a court case with Bulgarians because of land. Herbert Mauch also found it
  advisable to move away. He saw himself put at a disadvantage by the Blum group
  which continued to grumble even after Blum had left.

  In the year 1928 the congregation of Plotzk decided to add a tower to the
  chapel. This was caused by the fact that the belfry was in a bad state. When it
  had to be torn down, the bell was fastened on trees, to the laughter and
  ridicule of the outside world. Instead of rebuilding the bell tower, it was
  decided to build the bell tower next to the church. The joyful laying of the
  cornerstone was carried out by myself on July 29, 1928, using I Peter 1:25 as
  a text in the presence of a large congregation.

  In view of the schools, it must be stated that they were where the weirdest
  experiments took place. Besides the mentioned barbarism of the Russians during
  the time of war, there soon came (in 1922) the command that the village schools,
  as state schools, could only use the Rumanian language from Grade 1 on. The
  mother tongue was completely excluded. It took a lot of energy before the German
  population, especially through the action of Senior Pastor D. Hasse, was able to
  attain the permission for its schools to teach all classes in the German mother
  tongue. The exception being the teaching of history and geography which was done
  in the Rumanian language. However, this allowance is completely problematic,
  since at inspection time only the Rumanian language is considered. Even in
  subjects which should be taught in German the children are tested in Rumanian.
  This makes the teachers emphasize Rumanian more and makes German a secondary
  language. The German teachers show themselves actually as Rumanians. The
  teachers hardly regard the outward appearance of the children as something to be
  noticed.  For instance, the girls seldom comb their hair. Only when lady
  teachers were employed was there a change in this situation.

  In the prayer meetings of the brethren, there is for the most part, a
  self-righteous and self-satisfied spirit. However it cannot be denied, that also
  real Christians are to be found there.  It is sad that there is not more of a
  following in the prayer meetings of the brethren. For the meditation upon the
  Lord God which is cultivated in the meetings has a certain salvatory influence
  on all who take part. May God soon allow his spirit to blow so that conversions
  again take place in greater numbers!

  In connection with the views of the brethren gatherings we find the views in
  regard to raiment, especially that of women. Eighteen years ago it was unheard
  of that a woman or girl wear a coat during the cold time of the year. Outside, a
  large piece of cloth was used as protection from the worst cold. In church, one
  saw all women and girls covered up with a shawl. The skirts were long and wide,
  the blouses tight. A large apron mostly out of stripped Percale completed the
  women dress (costume). The girl's dress was the same only that the apron was
  missing. The men wore clothing made from un-died wool according to German
  fashion. In winter they had short leather coats. When it was much below
  freezing, lambskin coats, sometimes covered with a black cloth. The clothing
  mentioned here were considered appropriate by the meetings (of the brethren).
  However, before the war the dress changed. Fashion forced its way in. With the
  men appeared clothing from factory weaves, at the least, the Sunday suit is now
  worn according to general fashion. Now there is hardly a girl to be found who
  doesn't own a coat according to fashion; short skirts and high heels are a
  common sight. Only the women and girls who go to prayer meetings are less
  influenced by fashion.

  As in the other parishes, so also in the Fere Champenoise parish, women's
  societies were formed, which has as their purpose the making of various handsome
  work which was then auctioned off to the highest bidder. The profit is used for
  good causes. Unfortunately these societies have a short life span, as for
  instance, Katzbach, Plotzk, Paris etc. Even in the  pastor's town, where the
  pastor's wife leads the society, it doesn't want to grow and develop. Partially
  it is the lack of desire on the part of the women included, partially the fault
  has to do with the weak support of the community in the purchase of articles
  made. It takes much effort to awaken interest and keep it awake.

  The year 1928 brought ~ total crop Failure. It was so bad that it was even rare
  in Bessarabia, the land of crop failures. Wheat was killed by frost. Barley was
  so shriveled that one harvested hardly 10-20 pud (160-320 kilogram) per hectare
  (2.47 acres). Corn was completely destroyed by the drought during the summer so
  that only the occasional odd ear would form itself. Late in the year the dire
  situation already became evident: many families were without food and
  unemployed. Everyone tightened their belts as much as they could. The government
  prepared itself to provide relief but nothing of that was noticed in the German
  villages. Hope increased when the rumor spread that the Senior Pastor D. Hasse
  would be able to obtain a large loan from Germany for the German villages. The
  rumors changed the views of the congregations. All congregations of the parish
  were now willing to elect the presbyteries according to the Tranyslvania church
  order. Something which they had previously absolutely refused to do. It had been
  reported that congregations which had not elected their presbyteries according
  to the order, would have no part in the hoped for loan. The elections were
  carried out in all the villages without any great problems.

  The year 1929 began with a difficult inheritance (past year). The lack of food
  after New Year grew to enormous proportions in many families: half of Fere
  Champenoise was starving, Paris and Katzbach were worse. In the first two
  aforementioned villages, the wealthier and better off part of the population
  opened food kitchens, in which the school-children of the needy families
  received lunch. Finally in February the long awaited help arrived. Senior Pastor
  Haase was able to obtain a five year loan in Germany. This was for the relief of
  the German villages in Bessarabia. The interest rate was 18% per annum which was
  transferred for payment via the Tranyslvania Credit Union in the sum of 60
  million lei. Our parish received 4,938,600 lei. Fere Champenoise I 949,400. Fere
  Champenoise II, the same, Paris 1,282,500, Katzbach 550,100, Dennewitz 658,800,
  Plotzk 397,700, New Paris 146,700. The government also provided wheat and
  barley, however in an uncleaned state.

  This winter was particularly cold and long. On February 10 it was minus 26 C
  and on April 5th it snowed so much that the layer of snow was 20 centimeters
  thick.

  In order to provide an overview of the situation in the parish Fere
  Champenoise, the following report on the church life from 1932 is added.

I Inner Church Life

 a. The worship life

  Church attendance in the year 1932 was in general satisfactory, especially when
  the pastor held the service. If there are complaints about worship services
  attended less well. There are certain reasons. For instance, when the lay
  preacher/teachers state that printed sermons do not keep the attention (of the
  people) as well, so that many stay away from worship. In bad and in cold weather
  the church attendance in villages where the church was unheated, for instance in
  Paris, was very low, Special church celebrations did not take place in 1932.

 b. The religious - moral life

  In this area, it must be stated that in some villages, as for instance in Fere
  Champenoise II and Plotzk, apathy and so called non participation was present.
  In other (villages) active life ruled. For instance Paris with 6 and Fere
  Champenoise I with 5 prayer/Bible study meetings, where the word of God was with
  more or less understanding, but with great participation and joy. Those outside
  of the prayer meetings usually are apathetic towards everything that has to do
  with religion or is associated with it. For many religious life consists solely
  of outward observances and therefore found a beleaguerment in 1932 where an
  offering should have been brought.

  The village political animosities had a bad influence upon the religious moral
  life. For instance in Paris, between the upper and lower part of the village.
  The disputed piece of land was only 1 hectar of pasture land. However, it caused
  much excitement in the village. Thanks to the efforts of the church council, the
  dispute could be resolved by the end of the year. In the same way, the land
  dispute between Paris and Fere Champenoise I went so far that both congregations
  no longer wanted to hold their common praiseworthy day of repentance as in
  earlier years on May 19, 1932. Only after much effort was an agreement attained
  on the night before the day of repentance. The Rumanian political party
  campaigns brought much unrest into our villages. Where the people signed the
  party (membership) list as for instance in Fere Champenoise II and Dennewitz.
  The poorer population allows itself to be entranced by communist ideas which are
  spread on the part of Jews and Baptists.

  A bad habit which threatens morals and which is widespread is drinking. Although
  few are drunkards, recently too much alcohol is often drunk. In Paris there are
  two wine bars, one of which is owned by a Jew. Besides this many deal in secret
  with brandy and wine! The community of Fere Champenoise I always hindered the
  opening of bars.

  To the sins against sex, the sporadic birth of illegitimate children may be
  mentioned - 6 in the year 1932 as with the no longer single couples, of whom
  there were 18 out of 62 marriages. One man committed suicide in Dennewitz.

  The stealing of field and garden fruit is a widespread amoral act, and is hardly
  viewed as a breaking of the commandments anymore. Also. the amoral view of not
  paying debts has entered from the outside. Coupled with this is the fact that it
  is no longer possible to enforce church tax.

Sects activity

  To be named are Baptist families in Fere Champenoise I. In Plotzk 1 family.

  Changes over to the Baptists did not take place. during the year 1932, however,
  one man who came here, became Baptist in Larjari.

  In Paris and Fere Champenoise I there are some people called Separatists, no one
  joined them.

  One girl from Dennewitz, left the village and became Orthodox.

II Outward Church Affairs

 a. Dues (church tax) varied during the year 1932. For the churches were 74,886
  lei. On the lay preacher/teacher age and schools 31,626 lei were spent.

 b. Extent and Membership of the Parish

  The parish consists of 6 congregations: Fere Champenoise I, (Aft Elft), Paris,
  Katzbach, Fere Champenoise II, (Neu Elft), Dennewitz, and Plotzk. To Paris
  belongs the preaching point New Paris (Deubal). The number of souls on January
  1, 1932 consisted of 3,144 males and 3,455 females, together 6,599 persons.

III Religious Instruction

 a. In School religious instruction took place in the individual classes for 2
  hours per week. This was taught by the respective teachers as a secondary
  subject with an average low success rate.

 b. The instruction for children was mostly attended regularly by the children. 
  Only in Paris was the children's attendance unsatisfactory. The success rate was
  in general not satisfactory as only very few children show interest in the
  instruction.
