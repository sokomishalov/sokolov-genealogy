History of Java High School

By
Fritz Clement, Class of 1919
Bruce Clement, Class of 1947

Writing a history of your own school is an enjoyable but difficult task
because it gives you the opportunity to relive some of the most
enjoyable parts of your life. Records have not been adequate, and we
have had to rely on back files of the Java Herald for much of the in-
formation. Most of the school records of those first years of the
school's history have been lost or misplaced.

While the City of Java came into existence in 1900, the history of the
school does not begin until 1901 when the first term of school was held
in a building that had been moved into town from a site between the
city and the farm now being operated by Paul Jacobs. The first term of
school was taught by Mrs. Philip Noble, and there were twenty-three
pupils registered for the first semester.

Among the first students were Ethel Sammons, Elizabeth Hughes, Grace
Corson, Archie Stewart, Alice Corson, Elsie Sorwied, Arie Cook, Bertha
Hall, Emma Fiechtner and Ida and Nettie Stoller. The pupils ranged in
age from five to eighteen years. On the Board of Education were James
Corson, Philip Noble and Volney Varnum. Mrs. Noble received $35.00 a
month as teacher.
  
In 1903 a frame school building was built on the site just two blocks
east of Main street near where the old Clement home was located and
remained there until 1921 when the present more modern brick building
was constructed. The building was a two story affair and was adequate
for the needs at the time. O. I. O'Reilley was in charge of the
advanced grades while Lisle Pratton had the primary grades.

In 1907, with the population soaring in Java, more classroom space
became necessary and an addition was built to the south part of the old
structure (see picture) and two years of high school was added. Freeman
Chute was the principal. There were only three high school students,
Elizabeth Hughes, Bertha Hall and Freda Hein.

In 1914 there were two students who completed high school courses,
Henry Cook and Vina Varnum. George Hicks and Philip Noble were
sophomores and Frances Stangl (Mrs. Raymond Lee) a freshman. Betty
Hughes, teacher, also taught the seventh and eighth grades. In 1919
with A. G. Savage as principal, Fritz Clement was listed as the first
graduate of Java High School.

The enrollment of the school increased year after year and in 1920 the
need for expanded classroom facilities became apparent; and, although
the building was remodeled, it was still not adequate to meet the
growing demand for more educational space. In an effort to meet the
pressure, a small two-room structure was built which was used for the
primary classes.

With E. C. Giffen, school superintendent as one of the leading
advocates, and the fact that the old building was becoming a dangerous
fire hazard, the people of Java began to make plans for the erection of
a new, more fireproof building. On March 29, 1921 the issuance of
bonds in the amount of $60,000 was approved for the erection of a new
brick building.

When the tally was counted on the vote to proceed with the bonds, 232
Java residents had voted. There were 170 in favor of the new school,
and 62 against. The community also took a vote on the site for the
school in April 1921. Two sites were considered, with 97 favoring the
south edge of Main street and 31 voting for a site north of the
railroad tracks.

Contractors for the building were Fred R. Comb Co. for construction,
$49,175, and A. B. Donaldson, plumbing and heating, $12,385. Carl W.
Clement, working under the general contractor, did the electrical work.

The new building was completed in December of that year and was 126
feet long, 66 feet wide with a full basement and two stories. The
building housed a gymnasium which was one of the larger ones in the
area at that time. It had seating for about 200 fans on a balcony over
the shower and dressing rooms. On the opposite side a stage was
constructed.

The school site at the south edge of the city comprised 15 acres. The
area also contained the city ball park, and in early years a tennis
court on the site of the present city park. Over the years many
improvements were added to the site. The grounds were landscaped, trees
were planted and lawns were seeded. The grounds west of the school are
now a public park and the ball park and park facilities are in daily
use in the summer months. Adequate playground space was also added
and improved upon over the years.

Basketball games were being played in the old Grand Theatre building
where the first indoor boys and girls basketball teams were born. The
first boys team was coached by Fritz Clement and the girls team was
coached by Genevieve Fargen of Flandreau.

In 1919-20 night school was offered, with E. C. Giffen teacher. Ages
16-21 were allowed to attend.

It was in 1920 that Java was placed on a three-year accredited list,
and a few years later Java High School became a four-year accredited
school.

Foreign languages were taught off and on over the school's history. In
the 1920's Latin was one of the languages taught. Other languages
taught over the years included German, French and Spanish.

In 1921 students at Java High published their own newspaper, called
"High School Pep". It was done in hand set type with a group of
students doing the writing and typesetting themselves at the school
house. It was a four-page publication with news stories, advertising
support, and even an occasional photo. Making up the staff for the
first issue were Tina Noble, editor in chief, Simon Gimbel, business
manager, Edward Kunz, assistant business manager, Grace Noble, athletic
editor, and Gretchen Clement, news editor. Assistants were Elizabeth
Noble, Ida Krause, Cecelia Krause, Olivia Krause, Bertha Stangl, Clara
Lange, Tillie Dufloth, Lena Mertz and Emma Wolf. The paper was printed
every two weeks, and each issue had a different editor, chosen from the
students working on the project. E. C. Giffen, superintendent, had
charge of the group, and was given the title of Director of Publica-
tions.

Typing came into the classroom at Java in the 1924-25 school year, when
four typewriters were purchased, and classes continued on through the
years.

When the age of computers arrived, the school responded by adding two
Commodore 64 computers in 1983, and this department has since been
expanded.

The school progressed smoothly in the new school building with E. C.
Giffen as superintendent. He took a keen interest in the students, both
high school and grade. He was the originator of the Play Day activities
for grade children and was responsible for the first boys and girls
basketball teams. He later became state superintendent of public
instruction.

In 1921 before completing the new building, boys and girls teams were
playing basketball in the theater building, and often the games were
followed with a dance or lunch. A group of high school girls formed a
club called "We Seven" and served lunch to the ball players. The teams
played towns as far away as Cresbard and Faulkton. The girls team was
very good. Typical was their win over Faulkton, 36-2. The team went
undefeated for many years. First games played in the new gym were
Saturday, January 22, 1922, when the boys and girls teams took on
Cresbard.

In 1922 school opened on September 1 1, classes having been moved to
the new building in January of that year. Enrollment was 48 in high
school and there were nine instructors, including Superintendent Giffen.

The first music performances in the school were held in 1922. The music
department at that time included performing instrumental solos, vocal
solos, duets and trios. Miss Emma Oswald was the music director.

Giffen was followed by E. H. Halvorson, who was superintendent in 1923
and 1924. Miss Marie Banse (Hicks) and M. C. Munson were also members
of the high school staff. M. C. Munson then became superintendent for
the 1925-26 school term.

A high school carnival was held in March of 1923, raising $216. In
April of 1923, while the old school building was being torn down, plans
were being made to build a tennis court at the new school site. In
connection with the schools close that year, the freshman class held a
wiener roast at Haux's grove, with faculty members as their guests. The
school picnic was held at Hiddenwood Creek. Baccalaureate, Senior Class
Night, a gymnastic exhibition and Commencement were also held in the
closing days.

G. B. Shimmon was superintendent in 1926, 1927 and 1928 and during his
tenure much work was done in landscaping of the school grounds. The
Java Maroons won their first Hickorv Stick conference title under the
tutelage of Bert Popowski, and the girls team resumed their winning
ways again.

A. E. Sullivan was superintendent in 1929, 1930 and 1931. It was during
his first year that Java High School students participated in the
dedication ceremonies at Mobridge for the famous Indian Bird Woman,
Sakajawea and historical data of the school was sealed in a steel box.

Various activities were seen in what were termed the depression and
"dirty thirties" years. In 1929 an all-school program replaced the
usual carnival--among those taking part were Vivian Bauer, Ervin Kunz,
Gust Kundert, Leota Krause, Arend Lakeman and Walter Wolf. Directors
were Erna Kunz, Ida Roberts and A. E. Sullivan.

In the spring of 1930 the first speaking contest and debates were held
in the school. Merton Bender went to the state extemporaneous speaking
contest at Vermillion, after winning the district event. Debaters
included Gust Kundert and Arend Lakeman. Plays and operettas were also
presented, with the entire school taking part in some of the programs.

In 1930 the Java Maroons won their first and only district title by
defeating Selby 15-14. Frank Heezen was the coach.

In 1931 the janitor's job at the school became so popular that there
were 15 applicants for the job. Bids ranged from $50 to $80 per month
and Fred Pregitzer was the lucky man at $70.

Frank Heezen was elevated to the superintendency in 1932 and stayed in
that capacity for five years. Heezen was a popular superintendent, and
the school progressed smoothly during his administration.

Dormitories were a major factor in the school history, bringing in many
students from outside the city area. Started in 1934, the girls dorm
was located in the Campbell Hotel on the east side of Main street, and
the boys in a room on the first floor in the west side of the school
building. The first year there were 16 boys and 12 girls in the dorms.
Dormitory cooks were secured from the relief rolls in Java. Boys were
housed in the Gershin home on Main street, until the preparation of the
room in the school building was completed. The dorms were closed in
1944 because improved transportation and the low number of students
needing the service made operation no longer practical.

In 1933 the first "J" Club was organized by Coach Roy Stewart. Members
included those lettering in major athletics, declam winners,
cheerleaders and editor of the Maroon and Gold News. The 13 charter
members were Harold Spiry, Ben Buckenberger, Herbert Fiechtner, Ida
Klein, Arthur Boehler, Alvin Oltmans, Walter Sauer, Herbert Sauer,
Edgar Aman, Fritz Krause, Otto Kundert, Adeline Lutz and Lydia Wolf.

Ephraim Renz came as superintendent in 1937 and was at the helm of the
school for two terms. Enrollment that year in high school was 102, up
from 92 the previous year. Staying in the dormitories were 45.

Renz was followed by Dale Summers, another popular administrator, who
stayed at Java five years, and left the teaching profession to become a
lieutenant in the U.S. Navy.

In 1938 the school began publishing their annual, named "The Pilot". In
the early years, the annuals were a "home job", that is they consisted
of mimeographed pages with the individual photos glued in place. Aaron
Wagner was editor-in-chief of that first effort. In later years, the
annuals were a printed hard cover bound book. and were renamed, "The
Panther".

In 1939 students were charged $1.75 per week for board and room to stay
in the dormitories. Enrollment was 92 in high school, with a good
percentage being tuition students. There were 17 girls and 35 boys as
dormitory students.

In December 1939 Rueben Wolf won two state titles, one in the WNAX
Radio Essay contest at Yankton, and taking first in the dramatic
division of the state declamation league contest at Howard.

In 1940 Della Bauer was selected as one of six students in the state to
read an essay on WNAX Radio, Yankton. In May that year four boys in the
music department went to St. Paul, Minnesota to compete in the National
Regional Music Contest, as a quartet. They were Ervin and Roman Walz,
Ernest Flemmer and Emanuel Stickel. Their director was Leonard Stehr.

The fall of 1942 saw what was probably record enrollment, 103 in high
school. That year also saw the school selected as the local site for
signing up for gas rationing and other ration coupons during World War
II. Basic rationing limited driving to 240 miles a month, and speed
limit was 35 miles per hour. School time was also changed to "war time"
hours, 10 a.m. to 12 noon, and 1:15 p.m. to 4:15 p.m. In October 1942
I. R. Krause held a meeting for the purpose of starting a school band,
and 40 students indicated an interest. A "pep band" was organized in
1953 under full time director Clark E. Newman. New uniforms were
purchased in 1962 by the Band Mothers, and the Java band was taking
part in Gypsy Day and other events.

In the 1940's the school owned one bus that was used to transport the
students to out-of-town sporting events. It was driven by Gehbardt
Pfitzer. In 1958 a bus route was started to the north of Java which
picked up the students and brought them to school. In the 1960's a
complete route was started to pick up all the farm students. In 1962
four buses were purchased at a cost of about $6,000 to update the
transportation system. In 1983 a mini-bus was added to transport
football players to practice in Bowdle.

Harold Spiry (Class of 1933) became school superintendent in 1944 and
stayed with the Java school system for nine terms. There were 76 in
high school and 82 in the grades.

In 1946 the area to the west of the school building was improved and
made into a city park. That year the school session opened September 3.
There was talk of postponing the school opening because of a polio
epidemic in the state.

In May of 1948 Marilyn Jacobs was named Duchess at Northern Day at
Aberdeen, selected from 51 contestants. The following year Kenneth
Goetz and Kathleen Schlepp were chosen Duke and Duchess at Northern,
winning in a field of 47 high schools competing.

H. A. Peterson was superintendent in 1953-55; Harold Larson headed the
school in 1955-56, and D. J. Leighty was administrator for the 1957-58
school year. Gilbert Schlepp, a JHS graduate, served as band in-
structor from 1950-1957 on a part-time basis, winning many Division I
ratings at music contests.

A Parent-Teachers Association (PTA) was organized on October 5, 1955,
and was involved in fund raisers for purchase of items such as
basketball uniforms, copier, film projector, books, tape recorders,
etc. In 1970 the group changed to a Parent-Teacher Organization (PTO).
The group disbanded in March of 1981. Vern Cowley came to Java as
superintendent and coach in 1958; it was during his second term that the
Java school reorganization was approved by a 303-75 vote, and areas to
the south and to the north in Campbell County became a part of the Java
school system. Cowley gave up coaching in 1960 and devoted his entire
time to school administration, Kenneth Nesland taking over the coaching
duties.

Charles Singh came to Java in the fall of 1961 and stayed for two
terms. It was during his first term that Java approved a bond issue for
$160,000 for the completion of a new school auditorium and other
improvements on the building which included facilities for band and
other musical events. The auditorium seats 720.

In 1963 Gerald Nelson took over as administrator and was in Java for
two terms. The new auditorium was dedicated January 26, 1964. N. M.
Fisher came to Java in 1965 and stayed for only one term.

In 1966 Stanley B. Raabe became superintendent and stayed on for nine
years, leaving the system in 1975. Dean Stryker was superintendent for
the 1976 and 1977 school years. Orville Creighton came in 1978 and
stayed three years. B. J. Wasko to Java in 1981 and continues as
superintendent.

In 1974 Java music groups all took Division I ratings, band, mixed
chorus, girls glee club, boys glee club. Band director was Steve Serr
and vocal director Harold Spiry. Earlier that year students earned 11
Division I ratings for solos and small ensembles.

In 1975 soloists and groups including the band earned 15 Division I
ratings. Colleen Frank was chosen for All State Band in 1975 and 1976.

In 1978 Java was represented for the first time in All State Chorus.
Attending were Nancy Schreter, Charlene Goetz, Gavin Walz and Scott
Sauer. In 1979 Charlene Goetz, Bonnie Stangl, Donovan Stefanich and
Scott Sauer went to All State Chorus.

On March 27, 1978, the school honored Mrs. Reuben (Eileen) Schmierer.
She was presented with a plaque from the PTO for 30 years of teaching
and serving as elementary principal in the Java School System. Mrs.
Schmierer retired at the end of the 1985 school term.

A kindergarten class was organized in the 1980's. The school board
authorized the program March 13, 1980. That first year a 5 week session
was held with Mrs. Marvin (Betty) Pfitzer as teacher. In 1982 with
Janette Grenz as teacher there were nine pupils. Later sessions ran six
to nine weeks. In 1985 kindergarten was taught for a full semester for
the first time with a certified teacher, Marie Hirning of Eureka.

Kindergarten was also operated for a number of years in the mid 1960's.
The class was taught as an eight week session, with Mrs. Ernest
(Gloria) Zimmerman as one of the teachers during that period. Classes
were held in the school and for a time in St. Paul Lutheran Church.

High school enrollment dropped to 28 in the 1984-85 school year, less
than the state requirement of 35 for continued state aid. On October
24, 1984 a meeting was held to discuss the school's options--to
continue operation by raising taxes, contract out high school students
to neighboring schools, or close the school and merge with other
districts. A mailing to the district voters showed that 147 wanted the
school kept open, 97 wanted the upper grades contracted with the grade
school kept open, and 21 wanted the school closed.

With the enrollment below the state minimum of 35, $85,000 in state aid
would no longer be available to the district. Cost to taxpayers and
adequacy of the curriculum were the primary questions raised.

An election was set for January 22, 1985 to decide whether to continue
operating the high school, or to contract the upper four grades to
other schools. Of 417 registered voters in the district, 335 voted with
183 favoring closing the high school, 152 favoring continuing. The vote
brought about the closing of the high school on July 1, 1985, with the
students tuitioned to neighboring schools, and the grade school
continuing to operate.

There were six high school instructors in the final year. This book
contains information on 948 graduates of the high school in its 65 plus
years of operation.

As its final project, in 1985 the Java High School Band raised about
$5,000 and attended the three-day World of Music Festival at Edina,
Minnesota. They brought home a trophy and plaque.

The final graduation was held May 18, 1985, with Gust Kundert, JHS
graduate, as speaker. Final day for students of Java High School was
May 23. At that time 13 students planned to go to Selby, 11 to Bowdle
and two to Eureka.

This brings us up to date and we have tried to link together the past
and present. The future we will let take care of itself. We have
chronicled the achievements of some individual students in
extra-curricular activities (and there were many) where records made
mention of the event. Let it be said, though, that many from Java High
School entered district, regional and state competition and
distinguished themselves well and with credit to their school.

We know that when you read this book, see the class pictures and read
of the former grads, that vivid and delightful memories will be
recreated and all the pleasures of "school days" will come back to
memory and your reunion with those who shared the excitement and
pleasures of high school activities will be just what you thought they
would when you signed the registration which told us that you were
coming back "home" to be with us again for a few days. May this Java
Class Homecoming Reunion be another great highlight of all our lives.

Sports across the years

Football was played by students attending Java school in 1912-13. Since
none of the surrounding schools had teams, it was just a local
activity. A girls basketball team developed in 1914, but basketball did
not begin in earnest until 1920, when the movie hall was used as a gym
for girls and boys games. When the new school with a gym was built in
1921, interest in this activity increased.

We would be remiss if we did not mention the first Java girls
basketball team. From its organization in 1920, this team won 71 of 73
games played, and piled up a total of 1846 points against their
opponents' 406. Playing their first games in the old theatre building,
they really came into their prime in the new and larger gymnasium,
winning the state championship in 1922 and 1924. They were declared
state champions in 1922 after they were challenged by a team from
Carthage. The game was played at Aberdeen Normal (now Northern State
College), and Java won 32-2.

In 1924, the year the team had reached its peak, they entered the state
girls tournament at Mitchell and after breezing through Mt. Vernon,
Chamberlain, Harrisburg and Plankinton, met Lake Andes in the finals in
a great game. Java won 20-16 but Lake Andes forced them to go into
overtime to gain the win.

Most of the regulars were lost through graduation but the team again
entered the state tournament in 1926 only to be defeated in the opening
round by Howard 15-9. They rallied, however, for consolation play and
won from Farmer 34-3 and Bridgewater 21-9, then losing in the
consolation finals to Plankinton 17-9

Girls basketball was discontinued in the mid 1930's because the state
thought it was too strenuous for them.

The boys teams were not faring as well in the early years. It was not
until 1930 that the boys had a real winning team. They won the district
tournament that year and placed second in the regional. This feat was
never again repeated in the school's history.

In 1935 and 1936, the boys were runners-up in the district tournament,
and from then on, never finished higher than second in a district
tournament

In 1957 the boys took the Yellowstone Trail Conference tournament for
the first time. In 1969, Java won the conference championship and were
second in the conference and district tournaments.

In 1970 the Panthers were Conference tournament champions and went on
to win the YTC title. Java lost the district tournament by just two
points that year, losing to Gettysburg 49-47. The team was led those
years by Doug Schlepp and Steve Biel. Dan Besonan was their coach.
Schlepp scored 586 points his senior year and 1447 points for his four
high school years. He was selected as first man of the second squad of
the Class BAll State team. He went on to play basketball for South
Dakota School of Mines and Technology in Rapid City. He rewrote the
record book at that school, and set a state career scoring record which
stood for many years.

In 1974 the Panthers came through once more, taking the Yellowstone
Trail Conference tournament championship in a double overtime from
Bowdle. Bill Reidburn was the coach at that time.

In the 1976 and 1977 seasons, the Panthers were runners-up in the YTC
tournament.

Girls basketball was resumed in the fall of 1975, with play in the
Yellowstone Trail Conference, and participation in a statewide
organization. In 1979 the girls team was conference co-champions and in
1983 with Mark Schumacher as coach, the girls team won the YTC
tournament 67-61 over Cresbard. That year the Pantherslost in the
district tournament to Selby by one point.

In 1948 football began in earnest at the school. Five games were played
that first year, with 15 boys reporting for practice with Coach Lee
Bell. On that first team were Earl Anderson, Calvin Wolff, Richard Kary,
LeRoy Biel, Kenny Goetz, Marvin Hochstetter, Marlin Hochstetter, Curtis
Frank, Elmer Boschee, Robert Pfitzer, Jerald Pfitzer, Wallace Spiry,
Adrian Kast and Archie Aman. They won only one game, but learned fast,
as the following year they went undefeated, winning the 6-man
Hiddenwood Football Conference championship with six wins and no losses.

In 1974 the Java Panthers took the YTC football title with an
undefeated season. Java won their second YTC football title with an 8-0
season in 1976. Walt Simons was the coach, with Arden Gilbert assisting.
Dan Biel was named to the Sioux Falls Argus Leader All-State Team.

Because of declining enrollment, football was dropped in 1979, but
again resumed in 1980, when they played a 6 game schedule in 6-man
football. The 6-man play continued in 1981, and football then
discontinued for the 1982 season. The last two football seasons, 1983
and 1984, Java joined with Bowdle to play football.

Java track teams also distinguished themselves over the years. Some of
the students achievements included Pat Wolff going to the state
tournament in 1974, where he placed second in low hurdles.

In 1975 five boys from Java went to the state track meet, after winning
the YTC meet and the regional meet at Eagle Butte. Dan Biel, Doug
Schneider, Darrell Grenz, Glenn Neumiller and Pat Wolff attended. Four
girls also went to the state meet and were fourth in the state, placing
in four events. Going were Chloe Krause, Peg Ketterling, Kathy
Ketterling and Valerie Jacobs.

Java students kept up their good work in the summer of 1975, as the
Legion Baseball team won the State Class B championship.

In 1976 state track meet participants were Dan Schneider, Dan Biel,
Chloe Krause, Marlys Krause, Valerie Jacobs and Peggy Ketterling, with
all girls earning placings. The following year Dan Biel, Mark Melcher,
Peggy Ketterling, Chloe Krause, Marlys Krause and Bonnie Stangl went to
the state track meet, and all earned placings.

In 1977 Mark Melcher came into his own as a cross country runner. He
ran first in the regional and YTC cross country runs, and then took
first place in the state B cross country run at Brookings with a time
of 16:47. He was first in the YTC and regional cross country runs again
in 1978, and repeated as state B champion with a time of 16:28.

In 1979, Richard Fiedler, then 12, won a regional free throw contest
and entered state competition. On March 4 he won the state meet at
Chamberlain, making 22 of 25 free throws.

Lori Biel placed third in the state track meet in 1983 in long jump
with a distance of 16'2-1/4".

Many more students over the years turned in admirable performances in
various track events. The list of school track records follows, with
some of the records being set very recently, and some remain unbroken

Homecoming

The Homecoming celebration began in the 1950's after football began.
Until 1956, Homecoming and a school carnival were combined. The
carnival was started in the 1920's and then dropped for a number of
years. When it resumed in 1937, selection of royalty was added. Most
years a King and Queen were named, but occasionally just a Queen was
selected.

The carnival was discontinued after 1955, and Homecoming eventually
became a two-day celebration. Coronation and a program and pep fest
were held one evening, and a parade and football game the next day.
When football was dropped for a short time in later years, Homecoming
was held in connection with a girls basketball game.

50th Anniversary Observed -- Fourteen members graduated from the Class
of 1935. In June of 1985 they had their 50th anniversary class reunion
with eight members present. They believe they are the only class who
have graduated from Java High to hold a 50-year reunion. Three
classmates have died.

The officers were Orpheus Krause, president; Alvin Aman, vice
president, and Frieda Kast, secretary-treasurer.

Activities of the classmates in high school are remembered as follows:

Orpheus Krause, junior and senior plays, mixed chorus and glee club.

Herbert Schadler, basketball, track, glee club, mixed chorus, junior
and senior plays.

Alfred Biel, basketball, track, junior and senior plays.

Richard Schnaible, plays, basketball, mixed chorus and glee club.

Chris Pfitzer, student body president, junior and senior year; glee
club, mixed chorus, basketball and plays.

Verna Lutz, basketball, glee club, mixed chorus, tap dance team, band
and plays.

Viola Zweigardt, basketball, band, mixed chorus, glee club and plays.

Elsie Naasz, basketball, glee club, mixed chorus, plays and tap dance
team.

Susanna Wolf, cheerleader, basketball, glee club, mixed chorus, tap
dance team, plays and band.

Helen Horvath, glee club, mixed chorus and plays.

Frieda Kast, associate editor and editor-in-chief of Maroon and Gold
News, junior and senior years; plays, mixed chorus, glee club,
basketball and band.

The three deceased are Herbert Sauer, Gilbert Schlepp and Margaret
Eisenbeisz. They too, took part in glee club, mixed chorus, band,
basketball and plays.

Alvin Aman joined the CCC Camp three weeks before graduation, so did
not graduate with the class.

History Bits -- The Class of 1940 was the first class in Java High to
wear caps and gowns at graduation. Rueben Wolf was the Dramatic State
Champion. He received a superior rating, "The Button", which entitled
him to the divisional contest at Huron. Later he spoke at the state
contest at Howard where he received top honors. For skip day, the class
went to Bismarck and toured the newspaper, Mandan Reformatory school,
and some had their first plane ride. In music, the school won four
superiors at Mobridge in 1940. Instead of a prom, the class had a
Theatre Party. Also that year, the FFA won the state shop contest. The
dormitory was in operation, and the rising bell for dormitory students
rang at seven o'clock. Breakfast was served at eight after which the
students cleaned their rooms and made the beds. Dinner was at twelve
and supper at five-thirty. There were then free periods, times of
supervised study, and time for games. It was lights out at ten o'clock,
except on Wednesday nights, when students could report to the dean at
ten-thirty. Special permission was needed to stay out later. Both dorms
housed 45 students, 29 boys in the school building and 16 girls in the
hotel building. Mrs. Martin Gehrki was girls dean and Frank Manders the
boys dean. --From information furnished by Irene (Reuer) Permann.

Some Notes on the Class of 1946 . . .

"History has a way of getting away and with very few notes to guide,
memory will have to do the job. Good or bad--real or imagined.

"September 1934, the first grade of Java grade school was made up of 21
little ones. Through the years some left the class and others were
added."

"May, 1942, eighth grade graduation saw 15 of the original little ones
graduate. That fall 32 freshmen hit J.H.S. to probably make the past
and future students and instructors look and wonder."

"Through the next four years many accomplishments were made by this
group. Basketball - music -plays - declam - twirling plus mischevious
things such as locking H.F. in the shop among other things not so
nice."

"H. F. Spiry was the lucky one to be Supt. and Class Advisor for the
1946 seniors. Oh my!!! Many the time he held his head and remarked
'What am I to do with you people; what have I done to deserve this???'"

"Discussions were held about the class rings, class pictures, skip day
and more. The meetings were held for the various tfiings but probably
the longest was in planning of skip day. Class members were told other
classes went to Aberdeen. Class of '46 was different. Bismarck or
Minneapolis. The class finally agreed on Bismarck."

"Senior Class Play-- 'Your Face Seems Familiar' was as well received as
the previous year's Junior Class Play, 'Grandpa Goes To College'.
Marjorie Buechler took some of the pressure off of H. F. for that
period of time."

"Junior-Senior Banquet was held in the basement of the Lutheran Church
with the class agreeing on the motto, flower and color, much to H. F.'s
relief."

"On graduation day, 1946, 24 received diplomas and of these 24, 12 were
from the original first grade. Now to go on to bigger and better things
and make our mark in the world. After all, our motto was--is--and will
be, 'We Accept the Challenge'."

-- Harvey G. Teske
