Alexanderwohl

From: Woltner pp.131-2 (page 131)

This colony was founded in 1821 under the management of Mr. Fadejew, the chief
officer in the Contour for foreign settlers in Jekaterinoslaw and under the
leadership of regional manager Gerhard Ens (district mayor of the Molotschna
Mennonite area, 1821-1824) from Altona.

It lies on the left bank of the Behemtschekrak (Begim-Tschokrak) stream bed, 47
Werst from Orechow and 90 Werst from Berdjansk.

In low lying areas, the uneven ground consists of black topsoil while higher
ground consists of black earth mixed with clay. It is relatively well suited
for cultivation, cattle pasture, tree propagation; but produces little hay.

The congregation in question, which had already existed as a church for over
200 years in Prussia, emigrated to Russia, led by their church elder Peter
Wedel. When they set up a 2-day camp on the south side of Warsaw, the late
Emperor Alexander I drove out from the city to direct the maneuvers of a unit
of soldiers in the field.

Having been informed [of his imminent arrival] by some generals who hurried
past, we stood in anticipation as the Emperor approached, stopped the coach and
beckoned to us with his right hand.

Three of our church officials ran in his direction and were asked whence we
came and where we wanted to go. Upon learning that we wanted to emigrate to the
Molotschna in southern Russia, the Kaiser spoke: "I wish you a good journey.
Greet your brothers; I have been there." This occurred on September 14, 1820.

Upon our arrival in the Molotschna, our church elder Peter Wedel extended this
greeting in prayer houses before the gathered congregations.

The Contour in Jekaterinoslaw also had to be made aware of this remarkable
event; which chief officer Mr. Fadejew immortalized by naming the colony
Alexanderwohl, for he said: "Emperor Alexander wished you well (wohl)."

(page 132)

In 1821 22 families from District Schwetz in the Prussian administrative
district of Marienwerder settled here; followed in 1823 by 7 families and in
1824 by yet another family.

Before the arrival of the Germans the unsettled steppe had been leased by
Johann Cornies and used partly by the Nogai as cattle pasture and partly
cultivated by the Russians.

Among the settlers, 20 families received an advance from the Crown of 4104
Rubels, 28 4/7 K. silver. They brought with them ca. 8570 Rubels silver.

The year of settlement, 1821, was unfruitful and produced only enough grain to
reseed. 1882 was fruitful, but grasshoppers appeared and caused great damage
for 7 years.

1823 and 1824 were years of poor harvest.

The prolonged storm in the early months of 1825 caused the congregation to lose
many animals due to lack of feed.

Here [as elsewhere] the most difficult year was the "hunger year" of 1833.

The congregation [later] prospered by raising selected cattle and sheep,
introduced by the effective privy council Kontinius and the agricultural
association under the leadership of the unforgettable Johann Cornies.

mayor: Heinrich Voth
assistants: Heinrich Goerz, Jakob Schmidt
school teacher: Heinrich Buller
