  >>>>> Records of the Village of Kochanow, Russian Poland <<<<<

  Catholic Parish of Gluchow / Lutheran Parish of Rawa Mazowiecka

  South Prussia before 1806 / Duchy of Warsaw 1806-15 / Russian Poland
  after 1815

In about 1803-1804, a large contingent of Germans migrated eastward to the town
of Nowosolna (Neu Sulzfeld) east of Lodz.  Several lists of these settlers are
extracted in the Odessa library.

From that location, many of these people scattered to villages to the south and
east where they established permanent (or semi-permanent) residence.  Included
were numerous families that settled at Kochanow, near Gluchow.

Most of the settlers at Kochanow were cloth makers, some listed as master cloth
makers.  This village belonged to the archbishopric of Gniezno (Gnesen).  It is
not known if these Germans were invited to settle there by the Prussian
government in Berlin or if the archbishop was responsible for this.

After Napoleon's defeat in 1812, many families moved on to Bessarabia and the
Black Sea region.  Others of these Lutheran Germans stayed on. Warsaw Archives
(Georg. Dict. of 1880) indicates that in 1827, there were 71 houses and 462
inhabitants, most involved in the production of linen.  Church records indicate
that these were 99.9% German.  In 1829, the village became a state village and
therefore crown property, owned personally by the Czar of Russia.  After the
first major Polish rebellion of 1831, several families moved on to Volhynia.
[(source:  Robert Kunkel, professor and researcher in Warsaw, Poland]

Even though mostly Lutheran, the residents of Kochanow were obligated to
register their vital statistics at the Catholic Church in nearby Gluchow.  These
records are available for 1808 and later, all in standard Napoleonic paragraph
format.  Some early years have indexes.  Consistent indexing begins in 1814.  In
1828, the Lutheran Church in Rawa Mazowiecka was organized and most of the
Germans began to register there instead.  However, some scattered entries still
continue at Gluchow which was less than half the distance to travel.  The
Lutheran records of Rawa Mazowiecka have not been microfilmed and are believed
to have been destroyed in the wars.

The extractions below cover all Germanic (whether Lutheran or not) entries from
the Gluchow Catholic parish for the years 1811 through 1836.  The records for
1808 to 1810 are available.  However, they are not indexed.  Handwriting for
those years made extraction virtually impossible, at least for me.  If you know
a specific surname that you are looking for, it still may be possible to find it
based on recognition of letter patterns.  If you know that your ancestors lived
in Kochanow, I would encourage you to get the microfilm to see if you can find
further information in these early years.  Death and marriage records will often
show a very specific place of birth in Germany that could lead to many more
generations of data.

Many of the names in this list are Polanized so you will have to be very
flexible about the spelling.  The names are not linked to parents as they were
extracted from the indexes only.   Spouses are shown for marriage records.  If
you find someone of interest, you can go to the original microfilmed record to
get more information using the data regarding film, item, and page numbers.
