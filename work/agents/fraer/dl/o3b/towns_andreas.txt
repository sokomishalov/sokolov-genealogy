                        Dedication

We dedicate this book to the founders of St. Andrew's Church. In their desire
to have a place to worship and instruct their children built these churches.
Their vision, dedication, and labor gave us, their decedents, this opportunity
to celebrate one hundred years of uninterrupted worship. We thank God for
leading them in their endeavors and pray that He will guide us in the future as
we go forward. Also let us remember the hymn writer's words, which inspired
them to lay the foundations which we enjoy today.

EINS IST NOT

Eins ist not, ach Herr, dies eine,
Lehre mich erkennen doch,
Alles andre wie's auch scheine
Ist ya nur ein schweres yoch,
Darunter das herze sich naget und plaget,
Und dennoch kein wahres vergnugen erjaeget
Erlang ich dies eine, das alles ersezt.
So werd ich mit einem in allem ergoezt.
1697 Johann Heinrich Schroeder

(Translation into English)

ONE THING ONLY, LORD IS NEEDFUL

One thing only, Lord is needful;
Teach Thou me but this to know,
All things else, though fair appearing
Prove a heavy yoke of woe,
Beneath it the heart with its pining and fretting
Finds naught of true pleasure, new failures regretting
But one thing is needful, that I must possess
This one thing, for all thing, assures me redress.

1925 translation - H. Brueckner

PASTOR JACOB KOEPPEL

Pastor Koeppel, his wife and daughter. Pastor Koeppel organized St. Andrews
Lutheran Church and also dedicated the 1893 stone church on March 21, 1893.

Pastor Jacob Koeppel was born in South Russia on February 15, 1862. He received
his elementary education in a village school where a good foundation was laid
in the knowledge and understanding of the Lutheran faith His first home in
America was in Illinois where he also received his preparatory training for the
theological seminary of the former Iowa Synod. He completed his work at the
seminary in 1893 and that same year was ordained to the holy office of the
ministry.

During the years of 1893 to 1897, he did mission work at various places, where
several congregations were organized by him - one of which was St. Andrews
Lutheran Church.

From 1909 to 1912, he served congregations in Kansas and Nebraska. In 1918 he
returned to Dakota, then to Oklahoma. He retired in 1936. From 1940, he lived
with his son, Paul, at Chickasha, Oklahoma. He passed away on September 20, 1942
at the age of 81 years.

Pastor Koeppel will be remembered as a good teacher who was untiring in the
instruction of his confirmands. Always humble and unassuming, he was a good
example to all who knew him.

PASTOR OTTO BRUNTSCH

Pastor Otto Bruntsch served St. Andrews parish from 1898 to 1902

Pastor Otto Bruntsch was born January 31, 1874 in Meizen, Saxony, Germany. At
the age of 16, he became a member of a young men's Christian society and became
acquainted with the Mission at Leipzig. The Mission recruited young men for
their seminary. He entered the seminary on April 14, 1891. A six-year period of
preparation was realized. His medical doctor advised him that his health was
not constituted for the climate in Africa; therefore, he was advised to turn to
the Iowa Synod missionfield U.S.A. for admission.

They sent him to Eureka, South Dakota which was the center from which the
Dakota District obtained its missionaries for work in the missionfield. Brother
Bruntsch was sent to do missionary work in McIntosh County. Here, he met
Antonie Isaak, who became his wife on November 14, 1897. He served the
missionfield for four years.

Then he took over Jewell parish east of Ashley, North Dakota. From there he
founded the parish in Ashley in 1904, and built a church there. He also founded
the parish at Coldwater north of Jewell parish. He also served parishes at
Welcome, Minnesota; Menno, South Dakota; New Leipzig and Mercer, North Dakota.

For years he was the district secretary, correspondent to the Kirchenbote, etc.
At the time of his death, he was busy writing a history of the Lutheran Church
in the Dakotas.

Brother Bruntsch attained the age of 62 years. They had 10 children and five
preceded him into eternity. One child is buried at St. Andrews. In addition to
his wife, he was survived by five daughters, four grandchildren and one
brother, Professor Max Bruntsch in Meiszen, Germany. He is buried at Bismarck,
North Dakota.

PASTOR ALBERT R. SCHORMANN

Pastor Schormann served St. Andrews parish from 1908 to 1911. He was born Oct.
11, 1882 at Lemgo Lippe Detmold, Germany. Influenced early in his life by his
parents and the pastor who confirmed him, he aspired to render Christ full time
service. He entered Father Bodelschwingh's famed Colony of Mercy at Bielefeld,
and later continued his preparations at the Mission House at Breklum. He
emigrated to the United States in 1904. His first charge was with the
Evangelical Synod of North America. He later transferred his allegiance to the
Iowa Synod. He was sent to Quincy, WA for three years, then spent 18 months in
Vancouver, B.C. When the Iowa Synod decided to withdraw from home mission
efforts in the Pacific Northwest, Pastor Schormann followed a call to St.
Andrews parish. He spent four years in Hatton, Sask. where he founded three
congregations in the vicinity. From here he went to Rosthern, Calgary, Alta,
and then back to Hatton. In 1925, he accepted a call to Coeur d'Alene, Idaho,
then Missoula, MT. His last call was to Victoria, B.C. in 1930. He married
Marie Schneider on April 13, 1903. They had six children. He died May 2, 1940.

PASTOR HERMAN HENRY FRIEDRICH RIEKE

Pastor Rieke served St. Andrews from 1911 to 1912. He was born in Doehren, in
the province of Hannover, Germany on May 6, 1855. He received his ministerial
training in the missionary seminary of Hermannsburg, Germany. He came to
America in December 1884, and was ordained in Leban, Wisconsin. He married Anna
Maneke on Aug. 6, 1885, and the union was blessed with ten children. After a
busy two years of service in Cicero, WI, Pastor Rieke followed a call to the
far Northwest where he became one of the foremost pathfinders, particularly in
the states of Washington and Idaho. He served in the following parishes:
Genesee, Fairfield, Walla Walla, Odessa and Marcellus, WA, and St. Andrews
parish in Zeeland. He retired to Winlock, WA where he died on Nov. 8, 1919. One
of his children is buried in St. Andrews cemetery. Pastor Rieke was for
organizing congregations where the gospel would find a home and souls could be
drawn to the light of Christ. When he heard of any settlement of Lutherans, he
went there with his message. No distance was too great.

PASTOR ALBERT F. FREYMANN

Pastor Freymann served St. Andrews from Dec. 1912 to Dec. 1918 and again from
Jan. 1936 to Dec. 1953.

He was born Nov. 25, 1888 in Osnabruck, Germany. He attended Wartburg Seminary
at Dubuque, IA and was ordained July 16, 1911. His first calling was at Kasmer,
ND. In 1912 he came to St. Andrews parish for six years, then went to St. Luke's
parish in Wishek for 18 years. He returned to St. Andrews parish in 1936 and
stayed until 1953. He retired in December of 1953 and moved to Aberdeen, SD. In
1963 he moved to a nursing home in Belle Fourche, SD and died July 28, 1965.
Burial was at Belle Fourche.

PASTOR JOHANNES CARL HEINRICH GRAEPP

Pastor Graepp served St. Andrews from 1919 to 1921.

He was born March 6, 1860 in Stargard, Pomerania, Germany. He attended school
in Berlin. He immigrated to Canada in 1874. His theological studies were at
Muhlenberg College. On June 26, 1886 he was ordained in the Peters Church of
New York. His first charge was at Brockfort Mission, New York, then on to
Scranton, PA for three years. He served a parish in Springfield Corners, WI. In
1904 he joined the Iowa Synod, after which he accepted calls to Richford, WI,
Mannhaven, ND, St. Andrews at Zeeland, Ionia and Dysart, IA. He was married to
Maria Lutke and they had two children. His second marriage was to Luise Krause
and they had six children. Pastor Graepp spent 38 years in the ministry and
experienced some bitter labors. He and his family had many difficult times. But
his tireless helpfulness also won him many friends. He died April 17, 1925.

PASTOR JOHN SCHMIERER

Pastor Schmierer served St. Andrews from 1969 to 1972. He was born May 8, 1904
in Java, SD. He attended Eureka Lutheran College and graduated from Wartburg
College in Waverly IA and Wartburg Seminary in Dubuque, IA. He was ordained on
July 6, 1930. His first call came from Halliday, Dodge and Dunn Center in N.D.
He married Marie Bieber on Oct. 2, 1930. In 1933, they moved to Zap, ND. These
were difficult times. They were part time gardeners and farmers to supplement
their salary. Here they adopted two children. After a brief stay at McLaughlin
and Wakpala, SD, they returned to North Dakota and served six years at Golden
Valley and Dodge. Pastor Schmierer introduced English Sunday School and English
services, making the transition from German. He was also involved in founding a
home for the aged and was elected as the first president of the Beulah Joachim
Memorial Home. In 1946 he moved to Delmont, SD where he served his first
single-congregation parish for almost ten years. He then accepted a call to
Leola, SD, and from there they moved to Streeter, ND. In 1961 he received a
call to be chaplain and administrator at the Joachim Home in Beulah, ND. From
there they moved to Arnegard, ND where they ministered to the Wilmington and
Zoar congregations. Their next move was to St. Andrews where he ended his
46-year ministry. After retiring, he and his wife lived in Bowdle, SD.

PASTOR OTTO C. STEAHLING

Pastor Steahling served St. Andrews from 1975 to 1978. He was born May 4, 1913

PASTOR ROBERT D. BERG

Pastor Bob Berg was born Dec. 28, 1947 in Eau Claire, WI. He was raised in
Strum, WI, a small town in west-central Wisconsin. After graduating from
Eleva-Strum High School in 1966, he attended and graduated from the University
of Wisconsin-Eau Claire in 1970 with a double major in psychology and
sociology. On May 23, 1970, he married Roxanne Semingson of Eleva, WI. In
October of 1970, he entered the U.S. Army and served three years as a physical
therapy specialist. Two years of service were spent in Frankfurt, Germany,
where their oldest daughter, Melissa, was born in 1972. Returning from military
service, Bob began work as a field counselor for the Wisconsin Dept. of
Veterans Affairs Outreach Program, a program designed to help Vietnam Era
veterans. It was during this time he decided to enter Luther Seminary, but
delayed action on that decision for one year in order to fill the position of
District Supervisor for the Outreach Program. The seminary internship year was
spent in Silver Bay, MN where a second daughter, Michelle, was born in 1977.
After returning to Luther Seminary and completing requirements for graduation,
Bob was called to serve St. Andrews Lutheran (Zeeland) and Trinity Lutheran
(Kintyre, ND). He was there from 1979-1982. In 1982, he received a letter of
call from Trinity Lutheran (Cumberland, WI) and Georgetown Lutheran (Balsam
Lake, WI) and served there until 1990. In June of 1990, Bob accepted a call to
Chetek, WI Lutheran Church, where he presently serves as senior pastor.

Allan and Cheryl have two daughters, Karin Joy, born March 26, 1983, at

                History of St. Andrews Lutheran Church

St. Andrews Lutheran Church, a rural congregation, is located 13 miles
northeast of Zeeland, North Dakota, or 20 miles southwest of Wishek, North
Dakota.

The St. Andrews Lutheran Church beginning goes back to the year 1885. In the
fall of 1884, a group of Germans from Russia came to the United States. They
came as far as Menno, South Dakota (Dakota Territory) for the winter of
1884-1885. In the Spring of 1885 they headed north to McIntosh County, Dakota
Territory, settling in the Beaver Creek area where St. Andrews is located.

Being religious people of the Lutheran faith, the settlers worshipped in their
homes using the Bibles and Hymn books they brought with them from Russia.
Traveling pastors were sent out by the Iowa Synod of The Lutheran Church into
the new settlements to administer the sacraments, to perform marriages,
baptisms, administer communion and confirm the children.

In 1892, 15 families (charter) decided to build a church. Being very poor, they
built as cheaply as possible using architecture they had known and learned in
Russia. They used materials from the land they settled on. They used their
horses and wagons to transport sandstone which was located approximately 12
miles northwest of the site where St. Andrews is located. They purchased some
lumber for the floor and roof and also purchased some windows.

The structure is rectangular, 29' x 22'. The inside is 24' 10" x 18'. The walls
are approximately 24" thick and are constructed of sandstone held together by
mortar of clay, straw and water. Interior and exterior walls were originally
finished with a render composed of the same mortar mixture used to bond the
stone. Years later when commercial cement became available, the walls were
overlain with a cement mixture and whitewashed. The base of the walls are 2 to 3
inches thicker on the outside about 8 to 10 inches high, serving as a drip edge
which prevents rain from washing away soils directly beneath the walls.

A wood gable peaking 8' 8" high sets atop the stone walls. Its framework is
wood with narrow wood siding. An 8" drop board meets the sandstone walls,
slanting outward and downward, shielding the underlying walls from direct rain
wash. This kind of building is nearly extinct today. A five-sided wood entry
(Vorheusal) was added to the south side. Sometime in the forties, it was
removed as was the two-tier platform inside at the opposite end of the doorway
where the pulpit was located.

The church was finished and dedicated to the Lord by Pastor Jacob Koeppel on
March 21, 1893. Cost of the building was $385.00 and 15 days volunteer labor by
each of the adult male members.

The first known minutes were recorded March 3, 1893. The remaining membership
today are descendants of those first Germans from Russia settlers.

A new wood structure was built next to the original church in 1906 and
dedicated in 1907. This structure measures 26' x 60' and features a ten-foot
square bell tower entry which rises 60 feet to the steeple and cross. It bears
the classical form of eastern U.S. churches built during the colonial period.

This "Beacon on the Prairie" still serves as the congregation's house of
worship to this day. St. Andrews Lutheran Church was named to the National
Register of Historic Places on July 12, 1990. The church will be celebrating
its Centennial on June 12 and 13, 1993.

                        Glimpses from the Past

1885 - The St. Andrews Congregation was organized under the direction of Pastor
J. Koeppel. Services were held in the homes by traveling pastors sent out by
the Iowa Synod of the Lutheran Church. Some of the earliest records indicate
that these pastors came to the area 13 miles northeast of Zeeland, N.D.
approximately once a month and less frequently in the winter.

1885 to 1888 - Some of the early recorded baptisms:

Lidia (Lydia) Meidinger ...     June 22, 1885   Christian Amann ......  Apr. 24, 1887
Edward Fischer ............     Nov. 25, 1885   Friedrich Rudolf .....  May   ?, 1887
Jacob Meidinger ...........     Sept. 12, 1886  Jacob Gaub ...........  Aug. 12, 1888

1892 - Confirmation services were conducted by Pastor Theodore Hummel.

1894 - Confirmation services conducted by Pastor A. Mayer.

1895 - Confirmation services conducted by Pastor J. Koeppel

1896 - Confirmation services conducted by Pastor A. Mayer. Additional traveling
pastors were August Zimmerman, Otto G. Pett, Martin Reischauer and Gustave
Ehrenland. They performed services such as baptisms, confirmations, weddings and
funerals. In emergencies, when pastors were not available, these duties fell to
deacons and were later repeated when the pastors came through.

1892 - Oct 18. - Fifteen families met and decided to build a church at the
present site: Section One-Township 130, Range 73, in McIntosh County, North
Dakota, 13 miles northeast of Zeeland. Charter members were:

Adam Meidinger          Johann Pietz            Johannes Vossler
Freidrich Meidinger     Freidrich Thurn         Christian Maier
Johann Thurn            Georg Just              Georg Ketterling
Jacob Gaub              Christof Just           Johann Ketterling
Daniel Aipperspach      Friedrich Vossler       Adam Ketterling

The building was constructed of sandstone taken from a site 12 miles northwest
of where the building was to stand. Each member was to contribute 15 days of
labor, plus horses and wagon as required to haul the stone. Lumber for the
roof, floor and windows was purchased where available. The cost of the building
came to $385.

1893 - Mar. 3-Meeting conducted by Pastor Koeppel resulted in keeping the name
of the congregation "Sankt Andreas Gemeinde" (St. Andrews Congregation).
Mar. 21-Stone church was dedicated to the glory of God by Pastors Koeppel and
Raun.

1895 - A permanent Pastor (Koeppel) was called to serve the congregation at an
annual salary of $250.

1895 - June 30-Friedens Gemeinde (Peace Congregation), located six miles
northwest of St. Andrews, met with St. Andrews to request confirmation
instruction for their young people. It was agreed that they could attend
instruction and be confirmed with the children of St. Andrews. All those to be
confirmed must be able to recite from memory the Ten Commandments, the three
articles of the Apostle's Creed, the Lord's Prayer with its individual
petitions, the Sacrament of Holy Baptism and The Sacrament of the Altar (Holy
Communion) with all their explanations. In addition, a goodly number of bible
verses and hymns must also be known. All of this must be spoken in the German
language. A public oral examination was to be held before the rite of
confirmation was administered.
Holidays on which church services were to be conducted were 1st and 2nd
Christmas Day, New Year's Day, Epiphany, Maundy Thursday, Good Friday, Easter
Sunday and Monday, Ascension Day and Pentecost Sunday and Monday.

1895 - Membership had grown to 64 adults and 82 children.

1896 - Dec. 30 - A house was purchased from Pastor Koeppel for a parsonage and
moved to the church site. The cost of the home was $75. Later another house was
added on to the first one.

1897 - Aug. 15 - St. Andrews and Peace Congregations formed a two-point parish
to be served by Pastor Otto Bruntsch (on a temporary basis) at a salary of $300
a year. Since there were other congregations being formed in the area, it was
stated that if they decided to join the parish they must agree to abide by the
parish constitution in effect.

1898 - Feb. 15 - The newly-formed St. Paul's Church, located nine miles east of
St. Andrews, requested to join the parish. This made a three-point parish. The
pastor would serve them every third Sunday. In between services would be
conducted by the deacons with the reading of sermons from a Sermon Book written
by German pastors.

1898 to 1899 - Winter brought the need for hay and feed for the pastor's horses
(used in his travel to the congregations). The congregations were requested to
donate as follows: St. Andrews, four loads of hay; Peace, three loads; St.
Paul's two loads. Every family was also asked for one bushel of oats.

1900 - Parish treasury ran out of funds. It was decided to assess each family
25 cents. At the time St. Andrews had 31 families, Peace had 21, St. Pauls had
nine. The 61 families produced a working treasury of $15.25.

1901 - Pastor Otto Bruntsch was called to serve the Congregation. He served
until 1904.

1904 - The pastor's salary was set at $600 per year plus hay and grain for his
horses. Fees for pastoral duties were set as follows at the annual meeting: 50
cents for a baptism $1 per person confirmed, $3 to perform a wedding and $1 for
a funeral for members. For non-members, the fees were as follows: $1 for
baptism and $5 for confirmations, weddings and funerals $5. A newly-called
pastor should not be required to preach a trial sermon before being accepted.

1904 - Oct. 4 - A parish meeting was held to hear the St. Johns Congregation's
request to join St. Andrews, St. Pauls and Peace. St. Johns was located six
miles northeast of St. Andrews. The request was granted and now St. Andrews was
a four-point parish. St. Andrews was the central point, with confirmation
instruction held there for four weeks in June. The confirmation services were
held there also. This was most expedient, as having confirmation in each
individual Congregation would have involved too much travel on the part of the
pastor. The confirmands stayed with families closer to the main church while
receiving their instruction.

1905 - Pastor E. Moeckel was called to serve the parish. He served for two
years.

1906 - Pastor J. Melchert was called to serve St. Andrews' Parish. As the first
church became too small to accommodate the families of St. Andrews, it was time
to think about a larger building. On Jan. 6, it was decided to go ahead with
construction. A committee of three men was elected to do the planning. They
were Jacob Ketterling, Daniel Barreither and Friedrich Thurn. With the
assistance of the pastor, a wood structure was erected. It stood alongside the
first church and measured 26-by-60 feet and features a bell tower entry. The
polygonal apse (altar area) measured 22 feet in width by 12 feet in depth. All
furnishings were of oak, done in a Gothic design in this area. The interior was
finished with embossed metal on the walls and ceiling. An added feature was a
balcony held up by pillars on the north end of the church. This permitted added
seating space when necessary. The building was finished in 1906, but not
dedicated until the following year.

1907 - June 2 - St. Andrews new church was dedicated to serve God and the
surrounding community. Pastor J. Koeppel, St. Andrews' first pastor, preached
the dedication ceremony.

1908 - Pastor Melchert moved on to another church. Pastor A. Schormann was
called and accepted. Travel to the churches in the parish was still by horse
and buggy.

1911 - Pastor H. Rieke was called. He remained for two years.

1912 - Pastor A. Freymann, a new immigrant from Germany, was called and accepted.
He remained until 1918, when he was called to serve St. Lukes in Wishek. In
1936 he returned to St. Andrews parish to serve until 1953.

1914 - St. James (Jakobus), located 10 miles north of St. Andrews, requested to
join the parish. It was accepted, creating a five-point parish. This created a
very heavy workload for the pastor, making it necessary to conduct Sunday
morning and afternoon services on a rotating basis. Other services were
conducted by deacons, reading from sermon books in place of the pastor's
preaching. The order of services (hymns, Scripture readings, etc.) was outlined
in the German Lutheran Book of Worship, which was followed closely.

1919 - Pastor J. Graepp was called. He served until 1921.

1921 - Pastor A. Doering was called. He served until 1936. During his pastorate
a new parsonage was built.

1926 - A new parsonage was built at a cost of $758.13. It had oak flooring and
woodwork, with spacious rooms on two floors. Central heating made for
comfortable living.

1936 - Pastor A. Freymann returned to St. Andrews after serving St. Lukes at
Wishek for 18 years. He remained at St. Andrews until 1953, when he retired to
Aberdeen, S.D.

1936 to 1953 - Services and confirmation instruction were still conducted in
the German language, exclusively. Bible School for ages 5-14 was held for four
weeks in June. The pastor taught the confirmation group during that same period
while lay people taught the Bible School. When available, seminary students
were called to teach the younger students from the "Wartburg Fibel" (a book
which taught reading and writing in the German language and script). The older
ones memorized Luther's Small Catechism (all five chief parts) and read Bible
stories from the book "Bibelische Geschichte". In addition, they all learned to
sing hymns from "Unser Liederbuch" and memorized prayers for little ones from
the "Wartburg Fibel". The Bible School had no arts and crafts; just solid
learning in preparation for confirmation school later on.

1949 - May - Rural electric power came into the area through the KEM Co-op.
Previously, the parsonage was supplied by its own 32 volt power plant. Pastor
Freymann was serving the parish at this time.

1954 - Pastor John Hoyer was called. He accepted and served until 1958. The
parsonage was remodeled to include running water and modern appliances. The
floors were refinished and part of the porch became a half-bath, with the other
half becoming part of the living room. Pastor Hoyer organized a parish choir,
complete with choir gowns and singing in English. The American Lutheran Hymnal
was introduced for Sunday worship services. Sunday School and confirmation
instruction were begun in English. Mrs. Hoyer taught the first English Bible
School. As was only natural, this change-over resulted in some disagreements.
Some members left the parish to join churches in Zeeland and Wishek, only to
realize that the transition to English was coming or had already occurred
there, too.

1956 - Oct. 21 - St. Andrews celebrated the 50th anniversary of the
construction of the present church. Pastor Hoyer was serving at this time.

1958 - As farms became larger and people reached retirement age and moved into
surrounding towns, rural churches faced declining membership. The first church
to break from the parish was Peace (Frieden's) in 1954. Its membership was
absorbed mostly by St. Luke's at Wishek. St. Johns and St. James followed in
1958. St. Pauls left shortly thereafter, leaving St. Andrews as the only church
in what had once been a five-point parish.

1958 - Pastor Hoyer left and Pastor Henry Weber was called. Due to the ill
health of one of his children, he served only several months.


1959 - With more people moving out of the area and fewer members remaining to
carry on the cost of the church, a new partnership was created with Zion
Lutheran Church of Venturia, N.D. Since both congregations were small and only
17 miles apart, this seemed the expedient thing to do. The two-point parish
called Pastor Marvin Lapp, a new graduate of Wartburg Seminary in the spring of
1959. He served until 1968.

1969 - Due to declining membership, Zion of Venturia closed. This again left
St. Andrews as the lone surviving church. Pastor John Schmierer was called. He
served until 1972, when he retired to Bowdle, S.D.

1973 - During the summer of 1973, St. Andrews was privileged to celebrate the
80th anniversary of its organization and the building of the first church.
Pastor Schmierer conducted the celebration service.

1975 - Another opportunity to link with a parish arose when St. Andrews joined
with Trinity Lutheran of Kintyre, N.D. This arrangement lasted for 16 years,
beginning with Pastor Otto Staehling. He served from 1975-78. He lived in
Kintyre. Under his guidance, several changes took place at St. Andrews.
May 1975 was charter month for Boy Scout Troop #90. Members were Joel, Jeffrey,
Henry and Neil Dockter; David, Steven and Terry Ketterling; and Curtis, Neil
and Steven Meidinger. Leaders were Vernon Gerber and Larry Walth. The troop has
since disbanded.
The Ruth Circle of the American Lutheran Church Women (ALCW) was organized on
Dec. 2, 1975 with 10 active members. Officers elected were Mrs. Vernon Gerber,
president; Mrs. Ferdinand Schnabel, vice president; Mrs. Larry Walth, treasurer;
Mrs. Alfred Dockter; secretary; Mrs. Arndt Ketterling, stewardship secretary;
and Mrs. Walter Ketterling, secretary of education.

1976 - In conjunction with the nation's Bicentennial, St. Andrews congregation
was presented with a bronze plaque by the Zeeland Bicentennial Committee, in
recognition of its being one of the first churches in the area. The plaque was
mounted on the northwest corner of the original stone church.

1979 - Pastor Robert Berg was called. He accepted the call, living in Kintyre
and commuting to St. Andrews to conduct regular services. He served until 1982.

1982 - An innovation came to St. Andrews when a husband and wife team accepted
a call to serve the congregation. Pastor Allan Saugstad and his wife Pastor
Cheryl Matthews accepted a call to serve Trinity in Kintyre and St. Andrews in
Zeeland. They lived in Kintyre and served until 1986.

1987 - Another husband and wife team accepted the call from Trinity and St.
Andrews. Gary and Cathy Connors-Nelson came from California to serve their
first congregation in 1987. They lived in Kintyre, as the previous pastors had
done. The unusual thing about the husband and wife teams was that the women
pastors kept their maiden names and one of the men took his wife's maiden name
as part of his. The Connors-Nelsons served until 1991 when Trinity of Kintyre
joined with Trinity of Napoleon. St. Andrews was alone again.

1990 - July 12 - St. Andrews was named to the National Register of Historic
Places. This prestigious honor was made possible through the hard work of
members Herman and Ruth Thurn and Aaron and Katherine Meidinger. They spent
several years preparing the required information to qualify the entire unit
(both churches, the parsonage and cemetery) for the register. Lauren McKrosky
of the North Dakota Historical Society provided assistance with filling out the
many forms required. The Germans from Russia Heritage Society also added its
endorsement.
The building practices of the Germans from Russia immigrants, carried over from
their homeland, were an important factor in attaining the historic register
designation.
The cemetery, which is representative of the ethnicity of these founders, has a
unique burial pattern. There are no family plots. Adults and those confirmed
are buried on the south side of the cemetery in the order in which they died.
Infants and young children (not confirmed) are buried on the north side, again
in the order in which they died. Brought over from Russia, this pattern has
changed somewhat in recent years. For instance, if a surviving spouse wishes to
be buried next to the deceased, they may request that a space be left for them.
Another unique trademark of the Germans from Russia is a wrought iron gate on
the north side of the chain link fence surrounding the cemetery. This gate was
made and contributed in 1964 by former member Arthur Ketterling. It features the
scroll work seen in many of the Germans from Russia cemeteries in America. Many
of the cemeteries begun by members of this ethnic group also contain
gravemarkers made using this artform. However these are not evident in St.
Andrews Cemetery.

1992 - St. Andrews is presently served by Pastor Richard Speidel of St. Luke's
Lutheran Congregation, Wishek, on a regular basis and by other pastors in the
district as needed. The declining membership of St. Andrews has made it
impossible for it to maintain and support its own minister; therefore this
arrangement has allowed the services to continue indefinitely.

1993 - June 12 and 13 - And now this is the year of celebration for 100 years of
witnessing to the countryside and surrounding area; offering God's Mercy and
Protection in good times and bad.
The Cross of Jesus Christ at the top of the steeple is still proclaiming hope
and eternal security in a world of change and uncertainty. Always we must say,
"To God Be The Glory".

A MIGHTY FORTRESS
by Dr. Martin Luther, 1529

1. A mighty fortress is our God,
a trusty shield and weapon!
He is our help in every need,
That has us now o'er taken.

The old bitter foe,
now means deadly woe,
Deep guile and cruel might
Are his dread arms in fight,
On earth is not his equal!

2. With might of ours
can naught be done,
Soon were our loss effected
But for us fights the valiant one
Whom God Himself elected.

Ask ye "Who is He?"
Christ Jesus here see,
As Lord of Hosts adored,
Our only King and Lord;
He holds the field forever.
